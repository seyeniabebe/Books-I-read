About This "Collection"

This is not a book review site. It's a graveyard of emotional stability, a catalogue of the stories that crawled inside me and refused to leave. I built this archive because I was tired of pretending books are just "entertainment." Some of them broke me. Some of them rewired my brain. A few made me so angry I wanted to throw them across the room — and I still think about them years later.

Every entry here is a scar, not a rating. The famous lines, the gut‑punch summaries, the little marks of devastation — they're all attempts to capture what a book did to me, not just what it was about. If you've ever finished a novel and sat staring at a wall for ten minutes, you're in the right place.

This archive exists because beauty and misery are often the same thing, and I needed somewhere to put the wreckage.

Books I've Survived (So Far)

The Kite Runner by Khaled Hosseini

Rating: 9.5/109.5/10

Afghanistan, a wealthy boy and his servant's son share a bond that shatters, leading to a lifetime of guilt and redemption.
I sobbed — ABSOLUTELY WRECKED. The line "For you, a thousand times over" will haunt me forever.

“For you, a thousand times over.”

— Khaled Hosseini, The Kite Runner

A Woman Is No Man by Etaf Rum

Rating: 9.1/108.7/10

Three generations of Palestinian‑American women trapped by tradition, expectation, and violence.
I felt suffocated, then furious. The way silence is passed down like an inheritance broke me and opened my eyes.
This book screams, and I had to sit in the dark after finishing it.

“You are a woman now, and a woman is no man.”

— Etaf Rum, A Woman Is No Man

The Book Thief by Markus Zusak

Rating: 9.7/109.7/10

Narrated by Death itself, a girl named Liesel steals books in Nazi Germany while her foster family hides a Jewish fist‑fighter in the basement.
I ugly‑cried in public. The beauty of words against unimaginable cruelty left me breathless.
This book made me believe in the soul‑saving power of stories.

“I am haunted by humans.”

— Markus Zusak, The Book Thief

I Must Betray You by Ruta Sepetys

Rating: 8.8/108.8/10

1989 communist Romania. A teenager is blackmailed into becoming an informant, spied on by everyone he loves.
I was held hostage by the tension. It reminded me that silence can be a cage, and that the truth is a weapon.
A gut‑punch of a history lesson I’ll never forget.

“The truth is a weapon.”

— Ruta Sepetys, I Must Betray You
